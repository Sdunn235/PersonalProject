# Lucent Forge Climate, Hydrology & Biome Addendum v1 — Draft

**Proposed filename:** `lucentforge_climate_hydrology_addendum_v1.md`  
**Status:** Early draft / dependency document  
**Section IDs:** `§C1–§C8`

## §C1 — Dependency Rule

Climate and biome maps must be derived after:

- projection and latitude;
- elevation;
- ocean basins;
- mountain systems;
- broad atmospheric assumptions.

Do not paint horizontal biome stripes directly from latitude.

## §C2 — Climate Inputs

At minimum:

- latitude;
- elevation;
- distance to ocean;
- prevailing wind;
- mountain barriers;
- ocean-current temperature;
- seasonality;
- fantasy/affinity modifications.

## §C3 — Rain Shadows

Mountains create wet windward slopes and potentially dry leeward interiors.

This relationship should influence:

- forests;
- grasslands;
- deserts;
- glaciers;
- river sources;
- settlement potential.

## §C4 — Hydrology

Major rivers should:

- begin in wet or glaciated highlands;
- follow descending terrain;
- merge into larger systems;
- terminate in oceans, lakes, wetlands, or interior basins;
- split mainly in deltas or exceptional terrain.

Watersheds should be represented separately from visible river lines.

## §C5 — Closing Ocean Consequences

The Deep Ocean system may create:

- trenches;
- volcanic arcs;
- restricted currents;
- enclosed or semi-enclosed seas;
- high coastal seismic risk;
- unusual nutrient circulation;
- isolated marine ecosystems.

## §C6 — Biomes

Biomes are outcomes, not first causes.

The canonical map should support transitional zones rather than sharp arbitrary boundaries.

## §C7 — Affinity Interaction

Affinity may affect comfort, chemistry, ecology, material behavior, and local climate, but it must not become an excuse to ignore physical causal chains.

Distinguish:

- baseline physical climate;
- affinity-modified climate;
- cultural interpretations;
- gameplay approximations.

## §C8 — Deferred Work

No specific global wind cells, ocean currents, rivers, or biome placements are canonized here.
